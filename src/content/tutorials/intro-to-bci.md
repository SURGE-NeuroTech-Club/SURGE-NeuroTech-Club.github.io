## What is a Brain-Computer Interface (BCI)?

A Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) enables communication between the brain and an external device — such as a computer or robotic arm — without physical movement. BCIs allow users to control devices using their thoughts.

## How Does a BCI Work?

### The Brain and Its Signals

The brain is composed of billions of neurons that communicate through electrical signals. BCIs detect and interpret these signals, often using electroencephalography (EEG), which measures electrical activity through the scalp.

<details>
<summary>Learn more about neurons and brain signals</summary>

Neurons are specialized cells that transmit information throughout the nervous system. They communicate via electrical impulses and chemical signals. Each neuron consists of a cell body, dendrites, and an axon. Dendrites receive incoming signals, while the axon transmits signals to other neurons.

Neurons generate action potentials — brief electrical impulses resulting from changes in membrane potential. These travel along the axon to the synapse, where neurotransmitters are released to signal adjacent neurons.

EEG measures the synchronous activity of neurons, primarily in the cortex. This activity results in detectable electrical signals on the scalp, representing the brain's response to stimuli and cognitive processes.

</details>

### Producing and Reading Brain Signals

EEG signals arise from the synchronized activity of cortical pyramidal neurons when users focus on specific stimuli. BCI systems use machine learning algorithms to learn and interpret brain signals into commands.

<details>
<summary>Understanding electric dipoles and EEG detection</summary>

Pyramidal neurons in the cortex are key contributors to EEG signals. These neurons have long apical dendrites oriented perpendicularly to the cortical surface, forming electric dipoles when they fire.

The combined electric fields of many neurons create measurable potentials on the scalp. EEG electrodes placed on the scalp detect these potentials, recording voltage fluctuations over time.

Machine learning algorithms process the EEG data, identifying patterns associated with specific mental states or intentions — such as those arising from attending to a specific stimulus. This enables translation of brain activity into actionable commands for BCIs.

</details>

### Translating Thoughts into Action

Machine learning algorithms distinguish brain signals generated by focusing on specific stimuli from background noise, translating them into commands for device control. This enables users to perform tasks like moving a cursor, playing a game, or controlling a robotic arm solely through thought.

<details>
<summary>Exploring machine learning in BCIs</summary>

Machine learning algorithms in BCIs analyze EEG data to identify patterns (like event-related potentials or steady-state visually-evoked potentials) corresponding to user intentions. These algorithms are trained on labeled datasets to distinguish between random noise and when the user is attending a stimulus.

The most common technique is supervised learning, where models learn from labeled examples. Unsupervised learning identifies hidden patterns without explicit labels, and deep learning employs neural networks to extract complex features from EEG signals.

By continuously adapting to user inputs, machine learning enhances the accuracy and responsiveness of BCIs.

</details>

## Two Common Neural Responses Used for BCI

### Event-Related Potentials (ERPs)

ERPs are brain responses time-locked to specific events. They consist of components like the P300, which occurs about 300 milliseconds after an infrequent or important stimulus. In ERP-based BCIs, users focus on stimuli, and the system detects the P300 response to determine which stimulus the user is attending, and therefore which command to execute.

<img src="/images/tutorials/ERPs_new.png" alt="Example Event-Related Potentials - Tamara Bonaci, CC BY-ND" style="max-width: 60%; margin: 1.5rem auto;" />

<details>
<summary>Diving into ERP components and their significance</summary>

ERP components are labeled based on their polarity (positive or negative) and timing. Key components include:

- **P300:** A positive deflection occurring around 300 ms after an infrequent or significant stimulus. In BCIs, the P300 detects when a user is focusing on a particular stimulus (e.g., a letter in a matrix), translating that into a command.
- **N200:** A negative deflection associated with response inhibition and conflict detection. Useful for monitoring error detection or responses to conflicting stimuli.
- **N400:** Related to language processing and semantic incongruence. Can assist in language/communication applications.

ERPs provide insights into cognitive processes and brain responses, making them valuable for understanding attention, perception, and decision-making.

</details>

### Steady-State Visual Evoked Potentials (SSVEPs)

SSVEPs are responses to flickering visual stimuli, causing detectable brain waves at the same frequency. In SSVEP-based BCIs, users focus on a flickering stimulus to synchronize brain activity with its frequency, allowing the algorithm to determine which stimulus the user is focused on.

<details>
<summary>Delving into SSVEPs and frequency analysis</summary>

SSVEPs are generated when the visual cortex responds to stimuli flickering at specific frequencies. The brain's response frequency matches the stimulus frequency, enabling reliable EEG detection.

In SSVEP-based BCIs, users focus on one of several flickering stimuli, each associated with a distinct command. For example, if the user attends to a stimulus flickering at 10 Hz, the EEG signals show a 10 Hz oscillation, and the algorithm executes the corresponding command.

Frequency analysis involves decomposing EEG signals into their frequency components using techniques such as Fourier Transform and Wavelet Transform.

</details>

### Comparison and Considerations

- **Speed and Accuracy:** SSVEP-based BCIs generally offer faster and more accurate responses due to direct frequency matching.
- **User Comfort:** ERP-based systems may be less visually demanding — SSVEPs require focus on flickering stimuli, which can cause fatigue.
- **Signal Processing:** Both require advanced techniques to interpret user intentions from EEG data.

### Note on Hybrid BCIs

Hybrid BCIs combine multiple neural responses (such as ERPs and SSVEPs) to enhance performance and versatility. By leveraging the strengths of different neural signals, hybrid BCIs can provide more robust and reliable control in complex or dynamic environments.

**How Hybrid BCIs Work:**

- **Integration of Multiple Signals:** Hybrid BCIs simultaneously monitor different types of brain signals, combining the time-locked precision of ERPs with the continuous frequency information from SSVEPs. This cross-validation improves accuracy.
- **Increased Command Options:** Using both ERPs and SSVEPs, hybrid BCIs can offer a wider range of commands — an ERP might select a menu item, while an SSVEP determines how to interact with it.
- **Enhanced Adaptability:** If one signal type becomes less reliable (e.g., SSVEP detection declines due to user fatigue), the system can rely more on the other signal type.

<details>
<summary>Advantages and applications of hybrid BCIs</summary>

**Advantages:**
- **Improved Accuracy:** Combining multiple neural signals reduces errors by cross-referencing different types of brain activity.
- **Greater Flexibility:** Users can perform a broader range of actions from more diverse command types.
- **Resilience:** Hybrid BCIs maintain functionality even if one signal type is compromised.

</details>

Hybrid BCIs represent an exciting frontier, combining the best of multiple approaches to create more effective and user-friendly systems.

## Applications of BCIs

BCIs have transformative potential across fields:

- **Medical:** Helping individuals with disabilities control wheelchairs or prosthetic limbs.
- **Communication:** Allowing those unable to speak to convert thoughts into text or speech.
- **Gaming and Entertainment:** Creating immersive experiences controlled by thought.
- **Research and Exploration:** Studying brain functions and developing neurological treatments.

<details>
<summary>Exploring BCI applications in more detail</summary>

- **Medical:** BCIs restore independence for individuals with mobility impairments, enabling them to control assistive devices through thought alone — such as robotic prosthetics for amputees or individuals with paralysis.
- **Communication:** BCIs enable individuals with communication disorders to express themselves by converting brain signals into text or speech.
- **Gaming and Entertainment:** BCIs offer unique gaming experiences where players control in-game actions using their thoughts.
- **Research:** BCIs facilitate research into brain function and cognitive processes, aiding development of treatments for neurological disorders.

</details>

## The Future of BCIs

BCI technology is evolving rapidly, with ongoing research aiming to make systems more accessible and user-friendly.

<details>
<summary>Innovations and future directions in BCIs</summary>

- **Improved Accessibility:** Researchers are developing affordable, easy-to-use BCI systems for a wider population.
- **Enhanced Signal Quality:** Advances in signal processing and electrode technology aim to improve EEG signal reliability.
- **Integration with AI:** Combining BCIs with AI allows for more sophisticated signal interpretation, improving accuracy and speed.
- **Wearable BCIs:** Future BCIs may be integrated into wearable devices for seamless everyday use.

</details>

## Conclusion

Brain-Computer Interfaces merge neuroscience and technology, enabling direct communication between the brain and devices. They have the potential to revolutionize medicine, communication, and human-computer interaction.
